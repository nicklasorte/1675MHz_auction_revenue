**RE:** Auction revenue effects of license-area design for the 1675-1695 MHz band

## Decision requested

Which license-area design NTIA should encourage in its comments on the Commission's Further Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (FNPRM) in WT Docket No. 26-284. Comment deadline: [CONFIRM].

## Bottom line

All three designs raise about $4-5 billion for 20 megahertz before federal coordination. Option 3, Partial Economic Area (PEA) licenses with nationwide package bids, has the highest floor: in every case tested, it raises at least as much as the better of the other two options. Every estimate depends on three or more independent direct-to-device (D2D) bidders; with two or fewer, all three designs raise about $3.46 billion.

## Background

- **The proposal.** The Commission proposes two unpaired 10-megahertz blocks at 1675-1685 MHz and 1685-1695 MHz. Licensees could choose uplink, downlink, or time-division duplex (TDD), with a secondary mobile-satellite allocation for supplemental coverage from space. The Commission seeks comment on nationwide, regional, or PEA licenses and on nationwide package bids.[1]
- **The bidders.** We modeled SpaceX, Amazon Leo, and AST SpaceMobile as D2D bidders, and AT&T, Verizon, T-Mobile, and Ligado as terrestrial bidders. With two blocks, the third-highest bidder sets the price.
- **The evidence.** Prices come from FCC Auctions 97 and 113, the SpaceX purchase of EchoStar's adjacent 1695-1710 MHz licenses, and SpaceX's purchase of Grain Management's 800 MHz licenses.[2]
- **Two effects drive the result.** PEA licenses let each market go to its highest-value bidder: in Auction 97, price per person rose sharply with market size, so carriers win the largest markets. Against that, SpaceX paid 1.21 times the 2015 market-by-market price for a national footprint, a premium PEA-only licensing gives up.[3]

## Options

Gross bids, before federal coordination (upper bound). The base case uses the market-size effect fitted to Auction 97 prices. The lowest-tested column is the weakest result across the flat-price and 30% exposure sensitivities.[3]

| Option | UE uplink | D2D downlink | Lowest tested (uplink / downlink) | Main risk | Who must act |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Nationwide licenses | $4.07B | $5.14B | $4.07B / $5.14B | Gives up market-by-market gains (about $0.6B in uplink); shuts out regional bidders | FCC |
| 2. PEA licenses only | $4.65B | $5.21B | $3.46B / $4.24B | D2D bidders can't be sure of national coverage and discount their bids; down to $0.9B lost | FCC |
| 3. PEA licenses with nationwide package bids | $4.65B | $5.21B | $4.07B / $5.14B | More complex auction; many small bidders must jointly outbid a package, so packages can win too cheaply | FCC |

Option 3 matches Option 2 in the base case and matches Option 1 in the weak cases. It does this because a D2D bidder can bid for a national package, keeping its national premium without the risk of a partial footprint.

## Recommendation and draft comment language

**Recommendation:** [CONFIRM: recommendation]. Staff analysis favors Option 3 because it has the highest floor in every case tested.

**Federal equities:** The four National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) Data Collection System (DCS) receive sites the FNPRM proposes to protect. Under PEA licensing, the four PEAs that contain them hold about 3% of modeled uplink revenue, most of it in Baltimore-Washington.[3]

**Draft comment paragraph (Option 3):**

> In the FNPRM, the Commission seeks comment on whether to license the 1675-1695 MHz band on a nationwide, regional, or PEA basis, and whether to accept nationwide package bids.[1] NTIA encourages the Commission to license the band by PEA and to permit all-or-nothing nationwide package bids. NTIA's preliminary analysis indicates that this approach would let each market go to the bidder that values it most. It would also let operators that need a national footprint, including providers of supplemental coverage from space, bid for one without the risk of winning only part of it. PEA licensing would also let the Commission apply coordination requirements around the NOAA DCS receive sites to the affected markets rather than to the band nationwide. NTIA looks forward to working with the Commission as it develops the auction procedures for this band.

## Caveats and what would change the answer

- **Upper bound.** No federal coordination zones are modeled yet; every figure will fall when they are.
- **Bidder count.** With two or fewer independent D2D bidders, all three options raise about $3.46B. The bidder list needs sign-off.
- **Market-size effect.** Option 2's base-case gain rests on 2015 prices set mostly by Dish-affiliated bidders. If price per person is flat across market sizes, Option 2 loses $0.6-0.9B against Option 1.
- **National premium.** The 1.21 premium comes from one deal, paid in stock, by one buyer.
- **SCS rights.** If D2D operators can lease supplemental coverage from space (SCS) rights instead of bidding, all options fall to about $3.46B. The adopted FNPRM text should settle this.

**Open verifications:** adopted FNPRM paragraph citations; comment deadline; EchoStar and Grain terms from primary filings; mapping of NOAA sites to PEAs.

**Flags:** NOAA equity statements need IRAC coordination before release. Revenue figures in a filing need clearance. Package-bidding design questions may warrant review by the Office of the Chief Counsel.
